About

About Me

I'm a PhD researcher in Natural Language Processing at Lancaster University. My research focuses on subjective annotation, human label variation and disagreement, and the evaluation of NLP systems, with applications in Arabic toxic language and hate speech.

I am particularly interested in how people can legitimately interpret the same language differently, and how NLP datasets and models can better represent this variation rather than treating it simply as noise. My broader research interests include annotation design, perspectivist NLP, disagreement-aware approaches, and ground-truth construction.

Ultimately, I aim to contribute to NLP methods and resources that more fairly and faithfully represent the diversity of human perspectives in subjective language tasks, particularly in Arabic.

Research

Research Areas

01

Subjective NLP

Studying NLP tasks in which language can support multiple legitimate human interpretations.

02

Perspectivist NLP

Exploring approaches that preserve multiple valid perspectives rather than collapsing subjective judgments into a single label or singular ground truth.

03

Annotation & Ground Truth

Investigating how annotation design and data practices influence the labels used to train and evaluate NLP systems.

04

Human Label Variation

Examining disagreement and variation in human judgments as meaningful information about subjective language.

05

NLP Evaluation

Interested in evaluation approaches that better account for subjectivity, uncertainty, and variation in human judgments.

06

Arabic Toxic Language

Applying these questions to Arabic toxic language and hate speech, including the role of cultural and linguistic context.
