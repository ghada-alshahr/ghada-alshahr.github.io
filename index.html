About

About Me

I am a PhD researcher in Natural Language Processing at Lancaster University. My research investigates how annotation design shapes ground truth in subjective NLP tasks, with a particular focus on Arabic toxic and offensive language.

I am interested in how decisions across the annotation pipeline — including data collection, label design, annotator characteristics, annotation guidelines, disagreement handling and aggregation — determine which human perspectives are represented in NLP datasets and which are lost.

Research

Research Areas

01

Annotation & Ground Truth

Studying how annotation choices construct the labels that later function as “ground truth” in NLP datasets and evaluations.

02

Perspectivist NLP

Exploring annotation approaches that preserve multiple valid human interpretations instead of collapsing them too early.

03

Human Disagreement

Treating disagreement as potentially meaningful perceptual variation rather than automatically reducing it to annotation noise.

04

Arabic Toxic Language

Investigating subjective perception, cultural context and annotation design in Arabic offensive and toxic language resources.
